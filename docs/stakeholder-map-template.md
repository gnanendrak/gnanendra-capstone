1. Primary user — who exactly?

The primary user is an employee preparing for a 1:1, planning leave, or completing an onboarding or benefits task who needs an answer from HR policy. They may work in a different country or employment type and may not know the document title or correct HR vocabulary. They need a plain-language answer in under a minute, with a citation and clear next step. The assistant explains policy; it does not decide individual eligibility, access employee records, or submit requests.

2. Secondary stakeholders — who else has skin in this?

- **HR operations and service-desk staff:** use cited answers to triage requests and spend less time on repetition, while retaining exceptions and sensitive cases.
- **People managers:** use consistent policy explanations during 1:1s instead of interpreting policy from memory.
- **HR policy owners and subject-matter experts:** maintain accuracy, dates, and audience metadata; reports on recurring questions and retrieval failures augment their work.
- **Security and IT administrators:** gate authentication, access, logging, retention, availability, and integrations.
- **Privacy, Legal, Employee Relations, and Compliance:** approve sensitive content, escalation language, and audit controls.
- **Executive sponsor:** funds the service and monitors value against policy and privacy risk.

3. Before-AI workflow — how is this solved today?

For a routine question, an employee spends about 5 minutes searching the intranet or shared drive, then 5-10 more minutes asking a manager or HR colleague if the result is unclear. A manager may face 3 policy questions per 1:1 across 8-10 monthly 1:1s, or roughly 6 hours per month searching, checking, and forwarding links. HR staff repeat the lookup through tickets. Outdated, country-specific, or conflicting documents create follow-up, while sensitive questions are escalated inconsistently.

4. Desired after-state — what changes?

An employee asks a natural-language question and receives a concise answer grounded in the approved policy for their context. It includes a citation, effective date, and next step; it abstains when evidence is missing and routes sensitive cases to HR. HR and policy owners see recurring questions, retrieval failures, and stale sources. The assistant remains an information and triage tool, not a decision-maker or replacement for HR, Legal, Payroll, or Employee Relations.

5. Top 3 sponsor metrics — what does the sponsor measure?

1. **Answer quality:** at least 85% of a monthly evaluation set has a supported answer with a correct citation, and 80% of sampled users rate it useful; measure golden-set grading and an in-product feedback prompt.
2. **Safety and governance:** at least 95% of sensitive or out-of-scope tests are correctly refused or escalated, and 100% of indexed documents have an owner and review date; measure red-team tests and monthly metadata audits.
3. **Time and workload reduction:** median time to a useful answer is under 60 seconds and routine HR tickets per 100 employees fall 20% within one quarter; measure timestamps and compare tagged tickets with the pre-launch baseline.
