# Hybrid Work Policy

## 1. Document control and policy intent

This policy is part of the Northstar People & Culture framework. Its purpose is to make decisions predictable, auditable and understandable while allowing managers to apply judgment to the facts of an individual case. A policy sets a baseline; it does not remove the need to consider the employee’s role, business context, local requirements, employment terms, risk profile and any approved adjustments. Managers should avoid creating informal rules that differ from published policy unless an approved exception or local requirement applies.

**Purpose.** Provide a team-level framework for combining remote and on-site work while protecting collaboration, client service, learning and control effectiveness.

**Owner.** People & Culture.

**Effective date.** 2026-01-01. **Version.** 3.0.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to Northstar employees unless a local employment term, collective arrangement or applicable law requires a different rule. Contractors and contingent workers may be subject to equivalent requirements through their contract or assignment terms. Managers are responsible for applying the policy within their delegated authority and escalating matters that require specialist review. The policy should be read together with the employee handbook and any specific policy named in the cross-references below.

## 3. Definitions

**Hybrid Working.** A planned working pattern that combines approved remote work and on-site work.

**Team Anchor.** A planned period or activity where colleagues are expected to be co-located for collaboration, learning, control or relationship needs.

**Business Critical.** A situation where client, operational, regulatory, security, control or service needs require activity at a specific location or time.

## 4. Roles and responsibilities

**Employees.** Understand the policy, provide accurate information, meet agreed requirements, raise questions early, and cooperate with approved processes.

**Managers.** Apply the policy consistently, explain decisions, keep material records, protect confidentiality, and involve People & Culture or another control function when the matter is sensitive, complex or outside delegated authority.

**Policy owner (People & Culture).** Maintain the policy, provide guidance, monitor implementation and review material issues or trends.

**Control functions.** Risk, Compliance, Privacy, Security, Payroll, Mobility or Legal may be involved where the subject matter requires specialist oversight.

## 5. Policy requirements

1. Hybrid working uses the remote-working allowance as its standard remote component.
2. A hybrid pattern must be agreed with the manager and should be documented.
3. Teams should use on-site time intentionally for collaboration, onboarding, coaching, client interactions, control activities and activities that benefit from co-location.
4. Managers may require attendance on site when a business-critical need arises, subject to reasonable notice where circumstances permit.
5. Hybrid working should not create unequal access to information, opportunities, meetings or development.
6. Hybrid arrangements may be reviewed when a role, team, business need or performance condition materially changes.

## 6. Procedure and decision workflow

Employees are expected to read the policy that governs the situation and raise questions early. A manager should explain the decision criteria, keep the relevant evidence, and record material approvals or refusals. When the matter is sensitive, involves a legal right, affects another employee, or could lead to formal action, People & Culture should be consulted. The goal is a fair process that can be explained later rather than an undocumented decision based on memory or precedent.

1. **Step 1.** Team discusses role needs and collaboration requirements.
2. **Step 2.** Manager proposes or approves a working pattern within policy limits.
3. **Step 3.** Employee and manager document anchor days, meeting expectations, remote location and review cycle.
4. **Step 4.** Team reviews whether the pattern supports service, learning, inclusion and control needs.
5. **Step 5.** Changes are recorded and communicated.

## 7. Exceptions and special cases

Exceptions may be considered only where the policy allows them or where local law, contractual terms or a documented business requirement requires a different approach. The approving manager must record the reason, duration and scope. An exception for one employee does not automatically create an entitlement for others.

## 8. Records, confidentiality and evidence

Records created under this policy should be accurate, relevant and stored in the designated system. Employees and managers should avoid keeping parallel unofficial files that contain sensitive information unless required for a legitimate business reason. Where an issue moves from an informal discussion to a formal process, the record should show what changed, who made the decision, the evidence considered, the action required and the next review point.

Where a decision relies on a request, approval, review, meeting, assessment or investigation, the record should contain the minimum information needed to show what was decided and why. Sensitive personal information should not be copied into general team correspondence unless there is a legitimate need to know. Requests for records should be handled through the approved privacy, records or HR process.

## 9. Monitoring and review

The policy owner may monitor adoption, exceptions, cycle times, recurring issues, appeals, employee feedback and control incidents. Monitoring should be proportionate and must respect privacy and local data-handling requirements. Material trends may lead to updated guidance, manager training, workflow changes or policy revision.

## 10. Operational guidance

- A software team schedules one collaboration day each week and uses remote days for focused delivery, while keeping a mechanism for urgent on-site attendance.
- A new employee is in their first month. The manager increases on-site time for onboarding even though the standard remote allowance remains available.
- A regulated operations process requires a physical control on short notice. The manager may require attendance for the relevant task.

## 11. Policy interpretation and escalation

A policy is most useful when employees can apply it to realistic situations. For that reason, managers should distinguish a rule from an example. An example explains how a rule may operate; it is not automatically a new entitlement or restriction. Where a question is not answered by this document, the employee should use the named escalation route instead of assuming that silence means permission.
For interpretation questions, employees should first contact their manager where appropriate and then People & Culture. Questions involving privacy, security, tax, mobility, regulatory controls or legal rights should be routed to the relevant specialist team. When two policies appear to conflict, the manager should not pick whichever rule is more convenient; the issue should be escalated so the applicable precedence can be documented.

## 13. Practical questions and answers

**Q: Does this policy create an automatic entitlement?**  
A: No. The answer depends on the specific rule, approval requirement, applicable employment terms and documented circumstances.

**Q: Who should I contact when the rule is unclear?**  
A: Start with the responsible manager where appropriate and involve People & Culture or the named control function for policy interpretation.

**Q: Does an example override the formal rule?**  
A: No. Examples illustrate how a rule may work and do not independently create rights.

**Q: What happens when local law differs?**  
A: The local legal requirement or applicable contractual term prevails to the extent required, with People & Culture support.

**Q: Can an approval be changed later?**  
A: Yes. An arrangement may be reviewed when circumstances or business needs change, subject to the applicable process and fairness requirements.

## 14. Operational note 1
In operating hybrid work policy, managers should first establish the facts and then apply the policy requirement that is actually relevant. For working arrangements, that means avoiding assumptions about intent, avoiding comparisons that are not like-for-like, and confirming the employee’s approved arrangement or role requirement before making a decision. A decision that cannot be explained in simple terms is often a signal that additional review is needed.

## 14. Operational note 2
Consistency does not mean treating every employee identically in every circumstance. Under hybrid work policy, consistency means using the same decision criteria, documenting genuine differences, and considering approved adjustments or local requirements where they apply. Managers should record why an exception was accepted or why a request could not be supported so that future decisions can be compared fairly without creating an accidental precedent.

## 14. Operational note 3
Where a matter overlaps with another policy, the manager should identify the primary process and then coordinate rather than running two contradictory processes. The purpose of the cross-reference section in hybrid work policy is to show which neighbouring policy may hold the detailed rule. People & Culture can help determine sequencing, ownership, and whether the same facts need to be recorded once or in more than one approved system.
