# Upskilling and Learning Development Policy

## 1. Document control and policy intent


Northstar uses proportionate, auditable controls. A policy states a baseline but does not remove
the need to consider the employee's role, business purpose, local requirements, employment terms,
security obligations and approved adjustments. Employees should raise questions early and should not
make a commitment first and seek approval later unless the applicable procedure expressly permits it.

Managers should use the same decision criteria for comparable cases and document genuine differences.
Approval of one component of a request does not automatically authorize every related component. When
another policy owns a specific rule, the manager should coordinate the processes rather than create an
informal workaround. Sensitive employee information must be limited to legitimate need-to-know access.

Records should be kept in the designated system. A complete record normally identifies the request,
decision, evidence considered, approving authority, effective period and next review point. Where a
matter is unusual or high risk, early escalation is preferable to interpreting silence as permission.

Examples illustrate how a rule may operate; they do not independently create rights or entitlements.
Where local law or a signed employment term provides a different mandatory requirement, the local
requirement or applicable contract prevails to the extent required.

**Purpose.** Provide a framework for employee learning, role-relevant upskilling, certifications, mandatory training, development plans, learning budgets and evidence of capability.

**Owner.** People & Culture + Learning.

**Effective date.** 2026-01-01. **Version.** 3.0.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to Northstar employees unless a local employment term, collective arrangement or applicable law requires a different rule. Contractors and contingent workers may be subject to equivalent requirements through their contract or assignment terms. Managers are responsible for decisions within delegated authority and for escalating matters that need specialist review.

## 3. Definitions

**Upskilling.** Planned development that strengthens existing capability or adds skills relevant to current or future work.

**Learning plan.** A documented set of development goals, activities, timing and success measures agreed by employee and manager.

**Mandatory training.** Training that must be completed because of role, legal, regulatory, security, risk or company requirements.

**Development budget.** The approved amount available to support eligible learning or development activities.

**Certification.** A formal qualification, professional credential or vendor certification that verifies a defined level of knowledge or competence.

## 4. Roles and responsibilities

**Employees.** Understand the policy, provide accurate information, obtain approvals before commitments where required, retain evidence and disclose changed circumstances.

**Managers.** Apply policy consistently, check business purpose, verify approvals, protect confidentiality, and escalate exceptions or high-risk cases.

**Specialist functions.** Finance, People & Culture, Learning, Compliance, Tax, Security or other designated teams provide specialist review and maintain controls where required.

## 5. Policy requirements

1. Employees are encouraged to maintain skills relevant to their current role and to build capabilities needed for future responsibilities.
2. Learning priorities should align with role requirements, business strategy, control obligations, technology changes, client needs or agreed career goals.
3. Mandatory training takes precedence over optional learning when completion deadlines conflict.
4. Managers should discuss learning and development during performance conversations and maintain an up-to-date learning plan where appropriate.
5. External courses, conferences, certifications or coaching paid from a development budget require manager approval before commitment unless a standing authorization applies.
6. Managers should consider business relevance, expected application, cost, time away from work and available internal learning before approving external spend.
7. Completion of training or certification does not automatically create entitlement to promotion, salary increase or a change in job level.
8. Employees should apply learning responsibly and share relevant knowledge with the team where appropriate.
9. Some certifications have renewal requirements; employee and manager should agree who owns renewal cost and whether the certification remains role-critical.
10. Learning records are maintained in the designated HR or learning system and should contain only information needed for legitimate administration.
11. Where an employee needs an adjustment to access learning, the company should consider reasonable adjustments under the Equal Employment and Inclusion Policy.

1. **Step 1.** Employee and manager identify a development need or mandatory learning requirement.
2. **Step 2.** Define the target capability and how successful application will be demonstrated.
3. **Step 3.** Check internal resources, approved providers and the development budget.
4. **Step 4.** Obtain manager and, where required, Finance or Learning approval before external commitment.
5. **Step 5.** Complete the learning activity and retain evidence of completion.
6. **Step 6.** Discuss application of the learning in the role and update the development plan.
7. **Step 7.** For certification or role-critical capability, confirm whether renewal or periodic reassessment is required.

## 7. Exceptions and special cases

- A mandatory regulatory course may proceed under the required compliance process even when the optional development budget is fully allocated.
- An urgent business need may justify an external course outside the normal planning cycle if the authorized manager approves it and the business rationale is documented.
- Employees can request learning broader than their current role, but approval depends on career relevance, business need, available budget and team capacity.
- Where a provider or course involves sensitive company information, the employee must confirm that confidentiality and security requirements can be maintained.

An exception must state the reason, duration, scope and approving authority. An exception for one employee does not automatically create an entitlement for others.

## 8. Records, confidentiality and evidence

Records created under this policy should be accurate, relevant and stored in the designated system. Employees and managers should avoid unnecessary parallel files containing sensitive information. Expense receipts, travel itineraries, learning records, assessments and personal circumstances should be handled under the applicable privacy and information-security requirements.

## 9. Monitoring and review

The policy owner may monitor approval quality, turnaround time, exception patterns, rejected transactions or learning requests, completion rates and control incidents. Monitoring must be proportionate and respect privacy and local requirements.

## 10. Operational examples

- An engineer requests a cloud certification directly relevant to the role. The manager assesses business relevance, cost and expected application before approving the spend.
- An employee completes a data course and then uses the new technique to automate a team task. The manager can record the application as development evidence.
- An employee assumes that passing a certification guarantees promotion. It does not; promotion remains subject to the Promotion Policy and approved role criteria.
- Two employees request the same course in the same month. The manager considers role priority, deadlines, coverage and the available learning budget.
- A mandatory security course conflicts with an optional conference. The mandatory course takes precedence.

## 13. Practical questions and answers

**Q: Does this policy create an automatic entitlement?**  
A: No. Entitlements depend on the specific rule, approval requirements, employment terms and circumstances.

**Q: Can a manager approve outside delegated authority?**  
A: No. Requests above delegated authority must be escalated to the designated approval level.

**Q: What if local law differs?**  
A: The local legal requirement or applicable contractual term prevails to the extent required.

**Q: Do examples create new rights?**  
A: No. Examples explain the rule; they do not independently create rights.

**Q: Can an approval be changed later?**  
A: An approved arrangement or claim may be reviewed or corrected when facts change, subject to the applicable process and fairness requirements.
