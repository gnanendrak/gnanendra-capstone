# Travel and Business Expenses Policy

## 1. Document control and policy intent


Northstar uses proportionate, auditable controls. A policy states a baseline but does not remove
the need to consider the employee's role, business purpose, local requirements, employment terms,
security obligations and approved adjustments. Employees should raise questions early and should not
make a commitment first and seek approval later unless the applicable procedure expressly permits it.

Managers should use the same decision criteria for comparable cases and document genuine differences.
Approval of one component of a request does not automatically authorize every related component. When
another policy owns a specific rule, the manager should coordinate the processes rather than create an
informal workaround. Sensitive employee information must be limited to legitimate need-to-know access.

Records should be kept in the designated system. A complete record normally identifies the request,
decision, evidence considered, approving authority, effective period and next review point. Where a
matter is unusual or high risk, early escalation is preferable to interpreting silence as permission.

Examples illustrate how a rule may operate; they do not independently create rights or entitlements.
Where local law or a signed employment term provides a different mandatory requirement, the local
requirement or applicable contract prevails to the extent required.

**Purpose.** Provide a controlled framework for business travel, accommodation, meals, ground transport, expense claims, approvals, exceptions and records.

**Owner.** Finance + People & Culture.

**Effective date.** 2026-01-01. **Version.** 3.0.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to Northstar employees unless a local employment term, collective arrangement or applicable law requires a different rule. Contractors and contingent workers may be subject to equivalent requirements through their contract or assignment terms. Managers are responsible for decisions within delegated authority and for escalating matters that need specialist review.

## 3. Definitions

**Business travel.** Travel undertaken for an approved business purpose such as a client meeting, conference, training or company event.

**Pre-approval.** Documented authorization obtained before booking or incurring a travel commitment where this policy requires it.

**Reasonable cost.** A necessary and proportionate cost that supports the approved business purpose and falls within the applicable policy limits.

**Expense claim.** A reimbursement request submitted through the approved expense system with the required evidence.

**Exception.** A documented approval to deviate from a standard policy rule for a defined business reason and period.

## 4. Roles and responsibilities

**Employees.** Understand the policy, provide accurate information, obtain approvals before commitments where required, retain evidence and disclose changed circumstances.

**Managers.** Apply policy consistently, check business purpose, verify approvals, protect confidentiality, and escalate exceptions or high-risk cases.

**Specialist functions.** Finance, People & Culture, Learning, Compliance, Tax, Security or other designated teams provide specialist review and maintain controls where required.

## 5. Policy requirements

1. Business travel must have a clear business purpose and, where required, pre-approval before non-refundable travel commitments are made.
2. Employees should use approved travel channels and preferred suppliers where available.
3. Economy class is the standard for flights of up to 6 hours; premium economy may be approved for flights longer than 6 hours where allowed by local booking rules.
4. Business class requires an exception approved by the employee's designated executive authority and should normally be limited to long-haul travel or a documented business need.
5. Hotel selection should be safe, reasonably located for the business activity and within the applicable city or country allowance.
6. Meals and ground transport are reimbursable when they are reasonable, business-related or necessary because of approved travel, and supported by required evidence.
7. Personal upgrades, entertainment, minibar charges, spa services, leisure extensions and costs for accompanying family members are not reimbursable unless a specific approved exception applies.
8. Employees must separate personal and business expenses when a trip combines business and personal travel.
9. Expense claims should normally be submitted within 30 calendar days after the end of the trip, unless local rules specify a different deadline.
10. Receipts are required where the expense system or local law requires them. Lost-receipt declarations may be used only through the approved process and must not be used routinely to avoid the receipt requirement.
11. Manager approval does not convert a non-reimbursable personal expense into an allowable business expense.

1. **Step 1.** Confirm the business purpose, destination, business activity and whether pre-approval is required.
2. **Step 2.** Obtain the required approval before making non-refundable commitments.
3. **Step 3.** Book through the approved corporate booking route or supplier where available.
4. **Step 4.** Keep itemized receipts, invoices and other required evidence.
5. **Step 5.** Submit the expense claim through the approved system, normally within 30 calendar days after the trip.
6. **Step 6.** Manager reviews necessity, reasonableness, supporting evidence, policy limits and any exception.
7. **Step 7.** Finance processes the approved claim and may return incomplete submissions for correction.

## 7. Exceptions and special cases

- An unavoidable operational or client requirement may justify a cost above a normal threshold when the required approval is obtained and the reason is recorded.
- Travel booked urgently because of a genuine business-critical event may use the emergency route; the employee should document why normal pre-approval was not practicable.
- Accessibility, medical or other legally protected requirements may justify a different travel arrangement and should be handled confidentially with specialist support.
- For combined personal/business travel, Northstar reimburses only the business-related cost that would have been incurred under a comparable business-only itinerary.

An exception must state the reason, duration, scope and approving authority. An exception for one employee does not automatically create an entitlement for others.

## 8. Records, confidentiality and evidence

Records created under this policy should be accurate, relevant and stored in the designated system. Employees and managers should avoid unnecessary parallel files containing sensitive information. Expense receipts, travel itineraries, learning records, assessments and personal circumstances should be handled under the applicable privacy and information-security requirements.

## 9. Monitoring and review

The policy owner may monitor approval quality, turnaround time, exception patterns, rejected transactions or learning requests, completion rates and control incidents. Monitoring must be proportionate and respect privacy and local requirements.

## 10. Operational examples

- An employee books a premium hotel because it is convenient even though an approved-rate hotel is available nearby. Convenience alone is not sufficient justification for reimbursement above the applicable allowance.
- A client meeting moves at short notice and the employee must change a ticket. The additional cost can be reimbursable when the change is business-driven and supported by the required evidence.
- An employee stays two extra days for personal sightseeing after a conference. Personal accommodation and leisure transport are not reimbursable.
- An employee has an evening meal during approved business travel. A reasonable meal cost may be claimed when it is within policy and supported by the required evidence.
- An employee loses a receipt. They should use the approved lost-receipt process and provide the required declaration rather than silently submitting unsupported expenditure.

## 13. Practical questions and answers

**Q: Does this policy create an automatic entitlement?**  
A: No. Entitlements depend on the specific rule, approval requirements, employment terms and circumstances.

**Q: Can a manager approve outside delegated authority?**  
A: No. Requests above delegated authority must be escalated to the designated approval level.

**Q: What if local law differs?**  
A: The local legal requirement or applicable contractual term prevails to the extent required.

**Q: Do examples create new rights?**  
A: No. Examples explain the rule; they do not independently create rights.

**Q: Can an approval be changed later?**  
A: An approved arrangement or claim may be reviewed or corrected when facts change, subject to the applicable process and fairness requirements.
